A pattern I found twice

The policy was in the prompt.
The failures were in production.

Two systems—consumer AI safety and enterprise email security—led to the same conclusion.

Scroll through the evidence
01 · The gap

A written policy is an intention.

Production needs an operating point.

At a consumer AI company, safety rules lived in prompts while the production detector matched messages against known violations. It was fast—but blind to patterns it had not seen before.

The hard question was operational: If we tighten enforcement, how many more violations will we catch, and how much clean traffic will we disturb?

Prompt edits could change behavior, but there was no stable measurement layer across recall, false positives, traffic, latency, and cost.

04 · The test

The obvious thesis did not survive the experiments.

I expected a universal policy model to transfer from policy text alone. I tested that assumption using 450K production messages and roughly 1,900 policy clauses.

Context helped

49.8 → 54.8%

Recall at 5% FPR

A stronger backbone helped more

49.8 → 59.1%

Recall at 5% FPR

Complexity did not

Elaborate token-level and policy-interaction models generally lost to a strong pooled baseline.

Customer data compounded

Pretraining won at 32 labels. A customer-specific model overtook it around 128 in the out-of-domain test.

Measured on specific production distributions. These findings support a product direction, not universal transfer claims.

05 · The thesis changed

The model is replaceable.
The control loop compounds.

A classifier gives you

A verdict from today’s data.

One model’s representation.

A benchmark score.

→

A policy deployment platform gives you

A score you can threshold.

Labels that improve the next model.

Monitoring, promotion, rollback, and an audit trail.

The durable advantage is owning the score, the labels, and the gradient pipe.
